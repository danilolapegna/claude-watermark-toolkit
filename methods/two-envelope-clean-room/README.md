# Method 2: the two-envelope clean room

The name sounds more elaborate than the method. You make two short briefs, close the source and draft from those briefs only.

## Envelope 1: facts and meaning

Write down:

- what the reader should understand or do;
- one atomic claim per line;
- evidence, examples and qualifications;
- names, numbers, dates, URLs and exact quotations;
- audience, format and length constraints.

Do not copy whole sentences unless they must remain exact.

## Envelope 2: your voice

“Write in my style” is too vague. Record things a writer can actually follow:

- short, long or irregular sentence rhythm;
- formal, conversational, technical or blunt register;
- connectors you naturally use;
- how much uncertainty you state;
- words and phrases you would never use;
- two short samples of your real writing, when privacy permits.

## Seal the source

Check both envelopes against the source, then put the source away. The drafting person or non-Anthropic system receives the two envelopes only.

If you are thinking that this is just a summary plus style notes, that is correct. The useful difference is procedural: the original wording is no longer available when new sentences are chosen.

## Final check

Reopen the source after the draft exists. Compare facts, not sentences. Restore lost qualifications, remove invented claims and read the result aloud.

Use [Rewrite Room](https://danilolapegna.github.io/claude-watermark-toolkit/) if you want the browser to guide the separation and run the local overlap checks.
